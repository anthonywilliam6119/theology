The Quran: Sunni Islam’s Multiple Gods?

By Anthony William · Published October 2026

In this post I will be quoting several posts from IslamQA.org, a prominent, reputable Sunni website, in respect to the Quran being Allah’s speech.

These answers prove that Sunni Islam has turned the Quran into a Christ-like figure by claiming it to be uncreated and a divine emanation from Allah.

This in turn makes the Quran another divinity besides Allah, in fact 114 divine beings united together as one since each surah is supposed to be uncreated as well. All emphasis will be mine.

Is the Quran created or uncreated speech of Allah?

A common question asked by those confused by the theological terminology is whether the Quran is created or uncreated.

The answer from prominent Sunni institutions is unequivocal.

Darul Uloom Deoband

Fatwa 915/818=B/1429 states:

"So all Sunnis, including the four major schools of jurisprudence and others among the later and earlier generations, agree that the Quran is uncreated." — Sharh al-Fiqh al-Akbar

However, calling the holy Quran eternal and uncreated means that the Word of Allah Almighty is eternal, which is an attribute of Allah and is associated with Him.

So far as written and recited words of Quran are concerned, these are the created ones and they are not the attribute of Allah, but they indicate to the Words of Allah.

The fatwa concludes with a warning: A common person should avoid venturing such philosophical discussions, for sometimes a man may fall in suspicion and doubt.

Is the Quran Created or Uncreated?

Maulana Nazrul Islam of Darul Ifta Birmingham provides further clarification on why this issue is considered so critical in Sunni theology.

Aqidah Tahawiyyah

“The Qur’an is the word of Allah that emanated from him without the modality of saying... Unlike human speech, it is eternal and uncreated. Whoever hears it and claims it is human speech has disbelieved.” — Aqidah Tahawiyyah, p. 12

The Qur’an is the speech (kalam) of Allah. The speech of Allah is an attribute (Sifat) of Allah, and the attributes of Allah cannot be created.

Fiqh Al Akbar

Imam Abu Hanifa writes in Fiqh Al Akbar:

“Allah’s attributes are eternal, they are not new or created. Whoever says the attributes of Allah are not eternal or they are created or have doubts regarding them, he is a disbeliever in Allah (kafir)... To use our words to say the Qur’an, our writing of it or to use our words for its recitation is creation but in its originality, the Qur’an is not creation.” — Fiqh Al Akbar, p. 20

Aqeedah on Speech of Allah

Mufti Muhammad Ashraf of Jameah Mahmoodiyah provides a detailed breakdown of the Sunni creed regarding the speech of Allah, addressing complex philosophical objections.

He explains that Sunni theology divides the concept of speech into two distinct categories to resolve the dilemma of how an eternal God can produce a temporal book.

A Comparison of the Two Types of Speech

Concept Attributes Theological Implication
Kalaam-e-Nafsi
(Internal Speech)		 Ever-existing (Qadeem), uncreated, an eternal attribute of Allah. Denying its eternal nature is considered disbelief (kufr) by classical Sunni scholars.
Kalaam-e-Lafzi
(Expressed Speech)		 Haadith (newly created), temporal, consisting of physical sounds, letters, and materials. While the physical book is created, it points to and indicates the uncreated Words of Allah.

The Voice Moosa Heard and the Tree

The fatwa addresses a specific theological dilemma: How did Allah speak to Moosa if His speech is internal and uncreated?

The answer provided is that the speech of Allah with Moosa is Qadeem (ever existing), but the voice and print form is Haadith (a creation).

"The voice Moosa heard was from a tree. Allah had created the voice therein for it to denote on His actual speech. This voice was Haadith and part of His creation. It was not His personal voice." — Sharhul Aqaaid An-Nasafiyyah, Page 62

The scholar compares this to music: a person tunes his voice to a tune. The tune is not the voice of the man, but it denotes meaningful speech.

The Historical Context of the Debate

Interestingly, the scholars themselves advise against diving too deeply into these philosophical mechanics.

Mufti Muhammad Ashraf notes that such topics were not prevalent during the age of the Sahabah (companions of the Prophet).

He attributes the complex breakdown of Kalaam-e-Nafsi and Kalaam-e-Lafzi to the Mu’tazilah sect, who misinterpreted the issue in light of Greek philosophy.

Initially, the Muhadditheen went only this far to assert: "The Qur’an is the speech of Allah, not creation."

But when people started claiming that "the pages of the Qur’an and the ink is not creation"—which the Mu’tazilah mocked—the later theologians (Mutakallimeen) crystallized the issue into the two categories to defend orthodox beliefs.
