Resurrection

Did Jesus really rise from the dead? Historical evidence examined

September 18, 2026 · 8 min read · By Anthony William
← Back to all articles

The resurrection of Jesus Christ is the cornerstone of Christian faith. The apostle Paul put it plainly: "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile" (1 Corinthians 15:17). But is there any real historical basis for believing it happened?

What surprises many people is that even scholars who are not Christians — historians, archaeologists, and New Testament critics — have reached broad consensus on four core facts surrounding the death of Jesus. These facts demand an explanation. The resurrection is the most compelling one.

Fact 1: Jesus died by crucifixion

This is one of the most well-attested facts of ancient history. Not only do all four Gospels record it, but it is confirmed by non-Christian sources — the Roman historian Tacitus, the Jewish historian Josephus, and the Babylonian Talmud. Modern medical scholars who have studied the details of Roman crucifixion confirm that survival was effectively impossible.

Fact 2: The tomb was found empty

The earliest Jewish response to the resurrection claim was not "the tomb was never empty" — it was "the disciples stole the body." This is recorded in Matthew 28:13. That response only makes sense if the tomb was, in fact, empty. Even Jesus' opponents did not dispute the empty tomb. They disputed the explanation for it.

"The most reasonable explanation for the empty tomb, the post-resurrection appearances, and the origin of the disciples' belief is the resurrection of Jesus."

— Dr. Gary Habermas, historian and philosopher

Fact 3: Many people reported seeing Jesus alive

Paul, writing within 20 years of the crucifixion, lists eyewitnesses — including a group of more than 500 people at once, most of whom were still alive when he was writing (1 Corinthians 15:3–8). This is not legend built over centuries. This is Paul inviting his readers to go and ask these people themselves.

Fact 4: The disciples genuinely believed it and died for it

People die for things they believe are true, even when they are wrong. But people rarely die for something they know to be a lie. The disciples went from hiding in fear after the crucifixion to boldly proclaiming the resurrection throughout the Roman Empire, at great personal cost. James, Peter, Paul — all died for this claim.

What does the evidence demand?

Alternative theories — that the disciples hallucinated, that someone stole the body, that Jesus merely fainted — all fail under scrutiny. The resurrection, as historians like N.T. Wright and William Lane Craig have argued at length, is the hypothesis that best accounts for all the established facts.

You do not have to accept it on blind faith. The evidence is there to examine. The question is whether you are willing to follow it wherever it leads.

"The historical evidence for the resurrection is stronger than the evidence for almost any other event in the ancient world."

— N.T. Wright, New Testament historian
