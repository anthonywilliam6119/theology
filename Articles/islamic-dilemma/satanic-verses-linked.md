## Introduction

Few teachings about prophethood in Islamic tradition have caused as much theological difficulty as the story of the "Satanic Verses," which many classical commentators connect to Quran 22:52. According to that reading, Satan interfered with what Muhammad recited, and God then cancelled what Satan had cast.

At first this may sound confusing. How can a true prophet of God be affected by Satan? Does it mean Muslims believe their prophet was evil? It does not. Muslims do not believe Muhammad served Satan. The question is a narrower one: what do Islam's own sources say about Satan's access to the prophets, and what do they say about Jesus?

Two clarifications at the outset. First, the story is disputed within Islam, and many Muslim scholars reject it as unauthentic. Second, this article is not trying to prove that Muslims are deceived or wicked. It examines what the sources record and what follows from the unique way they describe Jesus and Mary.

The Apostle John gives Christians a simple instruction:

> "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world." — [1 John 4:1 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20John%204%3A1&version=ESV)

So the question becomes: who is the true prophet, and how do we know?

## What Quran 22:52 Says

The Quran and its classical commentaries discuss this subject most directly in [Surah Al-Hajj 22:52](https://quran.com/22/52), and the story is tied to the recitation of [Surah An-Najm 53](https://quran.com/53). Quran passages are linked to Quran.com; hadith reports are linked to Sunnah.com; and biblical passages are linked to BibleGateway.

> "Never did We send a Messenger or a Prophet before you, but when he did recite the revelation or narrated or spoke, Shaitan (Satan) threw (some falsehood) in it. But Allah abolishes that which Shaitan (Satan) throws in. Then Allah establishes His Revelations." — [Quran 22:52 (Hilali-Khan)](https://quran.com/22/52)

Notice what the verse says and what it does not say. It says that Satan throws something in, and that Allah abolishes it and establishes his verses. It does not say that every prophet was "inspired by Satan." Muslim scholars also read the verse in different ways. Some read it as Satan casting doubt among the hearers of a prophet's message, rather than altering the prophet's own words.

## The Classical Story of the Cranes

Several classical commentaries, including Tafsir al-Jalalayn, connect this verse to the story that Muhammad, while reciting Surah An-Najm before the Quraysh, spoke words praising the three pagan goddesses of Mecca (Al-Lat, Al-Uzza and Manat). [Tafsir al-Jalalayn on 22:52](https://www.altafsir.com/Tafasir.asp?tMadhNo=0&tTafsirNo=74&tSoraNo=22&tAyahNo=52&tDisplay=yes&UserProfile=0&LanguageId=2) describes it this way:

> "...added, as a result of Satan casting them onto his tongue without his being aware of it, the following words: 'those are the high-flying cranes (al-gharaniq al-'ula) and indeed their intercession is to be hoped for'..." — [Tafsir al-Jalalayn on 22:52](https://www.altafsir.com/Tafasir.asp?tMadhNo=0&tTafsirNo=74&tSoraNo=22&tAyahNo=52&tDisplay=yes&UserProfile=0&LanguageId=2)

This is important because the commentary says the prophet was unaware that Satan had cast these words onto his tongue. In the same commentary, Gabriel later informs him, and the verse is revealed to comfort him.

It must be added that many Muslim scholars reject this story. They point out, among other things, that its reports are *mursal* (they lack a Companion of the Prophet in the chain of narration), and that it conflicts with the Islamic belief that prophets are protected in delivering revelation. The Christian discussion of the story therefore concerns what the classical commentaries record, not a claim that every Muslim accepts it.

## What Does "Satanic Inspiration" Actually Mean?

The title "Satanic Verses" does not mean that Muslims believe Muhammad consciously worshiped Satan, or that the Quran as a whole is Satanic.

Even in the classical account, Allah cancels and corrects what Satan cast, so the final revelation is preserved. The theological question Christians raise is a different one: if a prophet, in the moment, could not tell the words of Satan from the words of God, on what basis does the follower have certainty in that moment? The question is raised respectfully, and Muslims have answers to it (see below).

The biblical framework presents a very different picture of the Messiah.

## The Biblical Context: Tempted, Yet Without Sin

Scripture says plainly that Jesus was tempted by the devil:

> "Then Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil." — [Matthew 4:1 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%204%3A1&version=ESV)

It also says how that temptation ended:

> "Then Jesus said to him, 'Be gone, Satan! For it is written, "You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve."'" — [Matthew 4:10 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%204%3A10&version=ESV)

The New Testament is clear that Jesus was tempted but did not sin:

> "...one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin." — [Hebrews 4:15 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%204%3A15&version=ESV)

On the night before his death, Jesus said of the devil:

> "...the ruler of this world is coming. He has no claim on me." — [John 14:30 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2014%3A30&version=ESV)

(The Greek text says, literally, that the ruler of this world "has nothing in me.") And John explains why the Son of God came:

> "The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil." — [1 John 3:8 (ESV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20John%203%3A8&version=ESV)

So the Bible does not claim that Jesus was never approached by Satan. It claims that when he was, the devil found nothing in him to use.

## Jesus and Mary: Protected from Satan

Islamic sources also set Jesus and his mother apart. The Quran records the prayer of Mary's mother:

> "And lo! I have named her Mary, and lo! I crave Thy protection for her and for her offspring from Satan the outcast." — [Quran 3:36 (Pickthall)](https://quran.com/3/36)

The hadith collections explain how this prayer was answered. Sahih al-Bukhari records:

> "There is none born among the off-spring of Adam, but Satan touches it. A child therefore cries loudly at the time of birth because of the touch of Satan, except Mary and her child." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 3431](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3431) (also [Bukhari 4548](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4548) and [Sahih Muslim 2366a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2366a))

Commentators such as Ibn Kathir cite this hadith when explaining Quran 3:36. Notice what these sources say. They do not say that nobody else was ever tempted. They say that, among all the children of Adam, only Mary and her son were exempted from Satan's touch at birth.

## How Muslims Respond

An honest discussion should include the Muslim answers:

- Many scholars reject the story of the cranes as unauthentic, and Muslims affirm that prophets are protected by God in delivering his message.
- Muslims believe Satan has no authority over God's chosen servants (see [Quran 15:42](https://quran.com/15/42)), and some read 22:52 as describing doubts cast among the hearers, not changes to the prophet's own words.
- A hadith in [Sahih Muslim 2814a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2814a) says that every person has a companion from the jinn, and that Muhammad said Allah helped him against his, so that he was safe from him and the companion commands only good.
- Muslims do not take the Mary and Jesus hadith to mean that Jesus is greater than Muhammad; they read it as a statement about the moment of birth.

A Christian reply is respectful but still asks a question. Even on these answers, the Islamic sources describe Jesus and Mary as set apart in a way that no other human beings are. The Bible says that this set-apartness belongs to the whole identity of Christ, not only to his birth, and that the unique protection points toward who he is.

## What Christians Do Not Mean

When Christians discuss this subject, it is important to say clearly what they are not claiming:

- Christians do not claim that Muslims worship Satan.
- Christians do not deny that Muhammad was a real historical figure who deeply shaped world history.
- Christians do not claim that Muslims are worse than anyone else; all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God ([Romans 3:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%203%3A23&version=ESV)).
- Christians do not claim that Mary is divine. The Christian claim concerns Jesus, not Mary.

The Christian discussion is theological. It asks what Islam's own sources say about Satan and the prophets, and what they say about Jesus.

## The Christian Position in Summary

- Quran 22:52 says that Satan casts something into the recitation of a prophet, and that Allah abolishes it.
- Many classical commentaries, such as Tafsir al-Jalalayn, tie the verse to the story of the cranes, although many Muslim scholars reject that story.
- The Quran (3:36) and the hadith (Bukhari 3431) say that Mary and her son were protected from Satan's touch at birth.
- The Bible says that Jesus was tempted yet without sin, and that the devil "has no claim" on him.
- This protection is consistent with the Christian claim about who Jesus is, but it does not prove that claim alone; the proof lies in the whole biblical witness to Christ.
- Christians are called to love their Muslim neighbors, respect them as individuals, and gently share the certainty of salvation found in Christ.

## Conclusion

The doctrine of the "Satanic Verses" should not be understood as the claim that Muslims worship the devil. It is a disputed account that raises a real question about how a prophet can be certain that the words he delivers are from God.

The New Testament gives a very different answer about Christ. He is the Word made flesh, the one whom the devil could not turn aside, and the one who came to destroy the works of the devil. The Islamic sources themselves, in setting Jesus and Mary apart from every other human being at birth, point toward the question the Gospels answer in full.

The differences here are not merely academic. They represent two competing claims about revelation, about the identity of God, and about the destiny of the human soul. Ultimately the discussion brings us back to the central Christian question:

Who is the prophet, and who is the Son?

Christians answer that God has spoken finally and fully through his Son ([Hebrews 1:1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%201%3A1-2&version=ESV)), and that those who test the spirits will find the truth in him.
