Isaiah 7:14 is one of the most important and most discussed passages in biblical prophecy. Christians have traditionally understood this prophecy as pointing ultimately to the birth of Jesus Christ.

But what did Isaiah mean in his original historical context? What does the Hebrew word almah mean? Why does the ancient Greek Septuagint use the word parthenos? And why does Matthew explicitly connect Isaiah 7:14 with the birth of Jesus?

01 — CONTEXT

The Historical Context of Isaiah 7:14

Isaiah 7 must first be understood within the political and historical crisis that existed during the reign of King Ahaz of Judah.

Judah was facing a serious military threat. Aram, also known as Syria, had joined forces with the northern kingdom of Israel against Judah. Their objective was to attack Jerusalem and remove Ahaz from the throne.

Isaiah 7:2 describes the reaction of the royal household:

Isaiah 7:2

“The heart of Ahaz and the heart of his people shook as the trees of the forest are shaken by the wind.”

The situation was therefore not theoretical. Isaiah was speaking into a real political and military crisis.

02 — AHAZ

King Ahaz and the Crisis Facing Judah

Ahaz belonged to the house of David. The political crisis therefore had significance beyond the immediate military threat.

God sent Isaiah to reassure Ahaz that the plans of the two attacking kingdoms would not ultimately succeed.

💡

The Central Issue

Isaiah's message was fundamentally a call for Ahaz to trust God's promise rather than fear the political powers surrounding Judah.

Isaiah therefore tells Ahaz to ask the Lord for a sign.

Ahaz refuses to ask for a sign, after which Isaiah declares that the Lord Himself will give a sign.

03 — THE SIGN

“The Lord Himself Will Give You a Sign”

ISAIAH 7:14

“Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and will call him Immanuel.”

The word sign is important. Isaiah is not simply announcing the birth of an ordinary child. The birth is presented as a sign connected with God's activity in the history of His people.

The identity and significance of the child therefore deserve careful attention.

04 — HEBREW

What Does the Hebrew Word Almah Mean?

עַלְמָה
almah

Hebrew term used in Isaiah 7:14

One of the most important linguistic questions surrounding Isaiah 7:14 concerns the Hebrew word almah.

The word refers to a young woman of marriageable age. The debate concerns whether the word itself specifically requires the meaning “virgin.”

It is important not to oversimplify the issue. Almah is not simply a technical biological term whose only possible English translation is “virgin.”

At the same time, the Christian interpretation cannot be evaluated by looking only at one Hebrew word.

The ancient Greek translation of Isaiah and the way Matthew later quotes the passage are also extremely important.

Key Point

The question is not only what almah can mean in isolation. We must also examine how ancient Jewish translators rendered the word and how Matthew interpreted the passage.

05 — SEPTUAGINT

The Septuagint and the Word Parthenos

The Septuagint is the ancient Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures. It was produced before the New Testament period and was widely used in the Greek-speaking Jewish world.

παρθένος
parthenos

Greek word used in the Septuagint translation of Isaiah 7:14

The Septuagint translates the Hebrew almah using the Greek word parthenos.

This becomes particularly significant because Matthew uses the same Greek word when he quotes Isaiah 7:14.

07 — MATTHEW

Matthew 1:22–23 and the Birth of Jesus

Matthew makes the connection between Isaiah 7:14 and the birth of Jesus explicit.

MATTHEW 1:22–23

“All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had said through the prophet: ‘The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel’ — which means ‘God with us.’”

Matthew does not leave his readers to discover the connection for themselves. He explicitly says that the event occurred in fulfillment of what was spoken through the prophet.

Matthew's Argument

The miraculous conception of Jesus is presented as part of the fulfillment of the prophetic Scripture associated with Immanuel.

08 — MARY

Mary and the Virgin Conception

Matthew explains that Mary became pregnant before she and Joseph came together as husband and wife.

Matthew attributes the conception to the Holy Spirit.

Luke gives a complementary account in Luke 1:26–38, where the angel Gabriel announces to Mary that she will conceive and give birth to the Son of God.

LUKE 1:35

“The Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. So the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God.”

The virgin conception therefore forms part of the New Testament's larger teaching about the identity of Jesus.

09 — FULFILLMENT

How Does Isaiah 7:14 Find Fulfillment in Christ?

Isaiah 7 belongs to a real historical situation involving King Ahaz. Therefore, its original context must not be ignored.

At the same time, Matthew understands the passage as reaching fulfillment in the birth of Jesus.

This means that the Christian interpretation reads Isaiah within the larger story of God's promises concerning David, Israel, the Messiah, and God's presence with His people.

11 — APOLOGETICS

Common Objections to Isaiah 7:14

Objection 1: “Almah does not mean virgin.”

It is true that almah can refer to a young woman of marriageable age and is not simply a technical word whose only possible meaning is “virgin.”

However, that observation does not settle the Christian interpretation. The Septuagint translates the word with parthenos, and Matthew uses that Greek wording when applying the passage to Jesus.

Objection 2: “Isaiah was only talking about his own time.”

Isaiah 7 certainly addresses a historical crisis during the reign of Ahaz. The historical context is essential.

However, Matthew explicitly interprets the birth of Jesus as fulfillment of Isaiah's prophetic Scripture.

Therefore, the Christian interpretation recognizes both the historical context and the later Messianic fulfillment.

Objection 3: “Matthew changed Isaiah.”

Matthew's quotation corresponds to the Greek form of Isaiah found in the Septuagint tradition. This means the connection between Isaiah and the language of virginity cannot simply be dismissed as a late Christian alteration.

Objection 4: “Isaiah 7:14 alone does not prove Christianity.”

Isaiah 7:14 should not be treated as the only evidence for the Christian faith.

The identity of Jesus is developed through the cumulative witness of the New Testament, including Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Paul's letters, and the broader biblical narrative.

12 — CHRISTOLOGY

Immanuel and the Identity of Jesus Christ

The greatest theological significance of Isaiah 7:14 is not merely the biological question surrounding the conception.

The deeper question is:

?

Who is this child?

Matthew answers by identifying the child with Immanuel:

GOD WITH US

John develops a similar theme through different language. John 1 teaches that the Word was with God, was God, and became flesh.

JOHN 1:14

“The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us.”

Matthew emphasizes Immanuel. John emphasizes the Word becoming flesh. Both passages present Jesus as uniquely revealing God's presence among humanity.

14 — FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Isaiah 7:14 refer to Jesus?

Matthew 1:22–23 explicitly applies Isaiah 7:14 to the birth of Jesus. Therefore, within the New Testament's interpretation, Jesus is presented as the fulfillment of the passage.

What does almah mean?

Almah refers to a young woman of marriageable age. The exact translation and implications of the word in Isaiah 7:14 have been debated.

What does parthenos mean?

Parthenos is a Greek word that can mean virgin. It is the Greek term used in the Septuagint's rendering of Isaiah 7:14 and is also used by Matthew in his quotation.

What does Immanuel mean?

Immanuel means “God with us.”

Was Isaiah 7:14 connected to the time of King Ahaz?

Yes. Isaiah 7 is directly connected with the political and military crisis during Ahaz's reign.

Why does Matthew quote Isaiah 7:14?

Matthew quotes the passage to explain the birth of Jesus as the fulfillment of prophetic Scripture.

Is Isaiah 7:14 the only prophecy Christians use for Jesus?

No. Christian interpretation also considers passages such as Isaiah 9, Isaiah 11, Micah 5, Psalm 2, Psalm 22, and many others, together with the New Testament's testimony about Jesus.
