Did Jesus Claim to Be God?

September 24, 2026 · By Anthony William
← Back to all articles

Few questions have generated as much theological and historical discussion as whether Jesus claimed to be God. The Christian answer is yes: Jesus is understood as the Son of God who became human. Jewish and other interpretations reject that conclusion, while historical scholarship distinguishes between the claims of the Gospel texts and what can be established about the historical Jesus.

Jesus did not use the exact words “I am God”

The Gospels do not record Jesus saying that precise sentence. The Christian argument instead considers the whole pattern of his words and actions: his authority to forgive sins, his relationship with the Father, his role in judgment, his authority over the Sabbath, and the way his followers understood him.

Claims of pre-existence and divine identity

“Before Abraham was, I am.” — John 8:58

Christian interpreters connect this statement with God’s self-revelation in Exodus 3:14. John’s Gospel also begins by identifying Jesus with the divine Word who was with God and became flesh. These passages are central to Christian belief in Jesus’ pre-existence and divinity, although their interpretation remains disputed.

Authority, worship, and forgiveness

In Mark 2, Jesus forgives sins and then heals a man, prompting the question of who can forgive sins but God alone. In John 20:28, Thomas addresses the risen Jesus as “My Lord and my God.” Christians view these passages cumulatively as evidence of divine identity. Other interpreters understand them as delegated authority or theological interpretation by the Gospel writers.

A balanced conclusion

Christianity teaches that Jesus is fully God and fully human, distinct from the Father but sharing the divine nature. Judaism does not accept that understanding of monotheism. Historians can examine the texts, dates, and early Christian beliefs; whether Jesus truly is God is ultimately a theological claim that goes beyond historical method alone.
